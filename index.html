Variability ↑
p tends to ↑

More spread increases uncertainty, making the signal harder to distinguish from sampling variation.

The key idea

Think of the p-value as responding to signal relative to uncertainty. Larger observed effects or greater precision generally provide stronger evidence against the null model. But random sampling means there is no guaranteed p-value for a given study setup.

*“Tends to” is intentional. Increasing sample size does not mechanically force the p-value downward in every new random sample. Teaching model: two independent normally distributed groups, equal SDs, and a two-sided large-sample comparison of means. The displayed 95% interval uses the same standard-error approximation. Real analyses should use methods appropriate to the study design, estimand, data, and assumptions.
