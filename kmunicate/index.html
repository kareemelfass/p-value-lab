KMunicate: see the information behind a Kaplan–Meier curve

Use the controls to change the same illustrative time-to-event data in both figures. The standard plot shows the estimated curves. The KMunicate format also shows uncertainty and the changing participant status behind the curves.

Why a curve alone can mislead

A late survival estimate can look stable even when few participants remain under observation. The curve does not, by itself, reveal how many participants are still at risk, how many have experienced the event, how many have been censored, or how uncertain the estimate has become.

Read the two figures together: they show the same data. What differs is the information made visible to the reader.

What the KMunicate format adds

Confidence intervals
The shaded bands quantify uncertainty around the survival estimate. They generally widen as the number at risk declines.
Participant status
The extended table separates participants who remain at risk from those with an event and those censored. It makes the information base of late follow-up explicit.

Use the format in R

The KMunicate package produces this reporting format from a survfit object.

library(survival)
library(KMunicate)

fit <- survfit(Surv(time, status) ~ treatment, data = dat)

KMunicate(
  fit = fit,
  time_scale = c(0, 6, 12, 18, 24)
)

Takeaway

The KMunicate format does not change the Kaplan–Meier estimate. It changes what the reader can judge: the uncertainty around the curves and the amount of information supporting each part of them.
